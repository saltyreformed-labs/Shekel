{# The tax-law notice (plan step salary:X-at-4; rulings salary:R-SAL74, R-SAL86, R-SAL87 and plan#3198). Rendered from base.html on every owner page when ``tax_law_gaps`` is non-empty: from November 1, when the app lacks next year's tax law, and from January on for as long as it lacks the current year's. The gaps (``app/services/tax_law_alarm.py``) come one per missing piece, by year, and each missing YEAR is one sentence pair, however many pieces it lacks (ruling plan#3198), so they are grouped here by year. Within a year the whole-year gap and the missing states are each picked out by their own filter on ``state``, so a sentence never rests on which gap comes first. ``gaps`` never gives one year both, because it judges a year it carries by its states and a year it lacks as a whole, so each year is one pair. A year missing states names them together, in the one wording of a list of states (the ``name_states`` filter, ruling plan#3202), and says their tax uses "older rules". A whole missing year says its paychecks and estimates use the newest year's rules -- the words ruling R-SAL87 approved -- unless a state is priced on a year older still (``priced_older``: the newest year itself lacks it); then it says "older rules" too, so no sentence names a year a line does not use. The weekly email names each part's year instead (scripts/check_tax_law.py). NO close button (ruling R-SAL87): the notice goes away when the law is added, and a dismissal would silence the one alarm that needs no email and no push to reach the owner. The id is on the outermost element so a test reads the whole notice, a close control anywhere in it included. The GitHub alarms read the same check through scripts/check_tax_law.py. ``fallback_year`` is none only when the law carries no year at all, which no release ships; the branch exists because a test installs such a law. #}
