{# Which year's tax rules priced a figure: the ONE spelling of the words (ruling salary:R-SAL75, "so the label is not built twice"; plan step salary:X-at-6). Usage: {% from "_tax_rules_macros.html" import rules_year_phrase, rules_note, rules_sentence %} All three read a TaxLawBasis (app/services/tax_config_service.py), the resolver's own answer carried on whatever was priced, so no screen works the year out itself: the Taxes tab hands them its liability's basis, and the salary pages a paycheck's tax lines' ``rules``. ``rules_year_phrase`` is the year alone when every part was priced on its own year's rules ("2026"); " on 's tax rules" when every part was priced on one other year, the app lacking the year asked for (ruling R-SAL76); and each part's own year when they differ. They differ for a year that shipped before its state's rules (ruling R-SAL86), and for every year after it while the newest year still lacks the state: with 2026 listing the state and 2027 not, 2028 is federal and FICA 2027's, the state 2026's. That case names the state, so the caller hands it the profile's ``state_code`` and the words never spell a literal one. ``rules_note`` is the sentence saying the app lacks the year's rules. Its words are modelled on the tax-law notice's ("Shekel doesn't have the 2027 tax rules yet", ruling R-SAL87) rather than copied: it says "2027's tax rules" where the notice says "the 2027 tax rules", and it drops "yet", which is false for a year before the law's first. It says "all of" only when some part was priced on the year's own rules (has_some_own_rules): 2027 above, never 2028, whose rules the app holds none of. It renders nothing for a basis that substituted nothing, so no caller can print the claim about a year whose rules the app has. ``rules_sentence`` is the two as one line of prose, "Tax year 2028 on 2027's tax rules. Shekel doesn't have 2028's tax rules.", for a page with no "Tax year" label of its own to put the phrase beside: the cockpit's "Where this paycheck goes" card and the projection ledger.
